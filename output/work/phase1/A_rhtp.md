## Federal/state funding: CMS Rural Health Transformation Program

### 1. What it is (verified)
- Statute: Section 71401 of Public Law 119-21 (H.R. 1, "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"; CMS calls it the "Working Families Tax Cuts legislation"), signed July 4, 2025. [https://www.medicaid.gov/resources-for-states/rural-health] [https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-announces-50-billion-awards-strengthen-rural-health-all-50-states] [MN NOGO: https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/hospitalnogo.pdf]
- Size / years: $50B total, $10B per year FY2026-FY2030; cooperative agreements with states only. [CMS press release above]
- Split: 50% distributed equally among approved states (so ~$100M baseline per state in FY26), 50% allocated by CMS on rurality metrics, state policy actions, and application impact scoring. [CMS press release] [https://pcghealthpolicy.com/2026/01/29/rural-health-transformation-program-applications-awarded-overview-of-state-funding/]
- Timing: NOFO opened Sept 15, 2025; single application window closed Nov 5, 2025; awards announced Dec 29, 2025 (all 50 states). FY27 award amounts to be determined by Oct 31, 2026, then Oct 31 each year through 2029. Budget period 1 is 10 months; later ones 12 months. [https://www.cms.gov/files/document/rural-health-transformation-frequently-asked-questions.pdf] [NOFO: https://apply07.grants.gov/apply/opportunities/instructions/PKG00291485-instructions.pdf]
- Spend deadline / clawback mechanics: states have until the end of the following fiscal year to spend each budget period's award; "unexpended or unobligated funds will be redistributed"; earmarked-but-unpaid subawards do not count as spent; anything unexpended as of Oct 1, 2032 returns to Treasury. State policy commitments must be met by Dec 31, 2028 or scoring (and future funding) suffers. [NRHA NOFO summary: https://nrha-prod-eastus-be.azure.silvertech.net/NationalRuralHealth/media/Documents/Advocacy/2025/RHTP-NOFO-summary-9-16-25.pdf]
- Eligibility / subawards: only the 50 states apply. "Local governments, hospitals, universities, nonprofits ... may still be able to participate through subawards or partnerships if their State chooses." "Organizations should follow the guidance of their Governor's office." [CMS FAQ above]

### Allowable uses (statute categories A-K, NOFO)
States must use funds for at least 3 of: A prevention/chronic disease; B provider payments; C consumer tech solutions ("consumer-facing, technology-driven solutions for the prevention and management of chronic diseases"); D training and TA "for the development and adoption of technology-enabled solutions that improve care delivery in rural hospitals, including remote monitoring, robotics, artificial intelligence, and other advanced technologies"; E workforce; F IT advances ("technical assistance, software, and hardware for significant information technology advances designed to improve efficiency, enhance cybersecurity capability development, and improve patient health outcomes"); G appropriate care availability ("right size their health care delivery systems"); H behavioral health; I innovative care / value-based; J capital expenditures and infrastructure; K fostering collaboration ("improve financial stability of rural facilities"). [NOFO: https://apply07.grants.gov/apply/opportunities/instructions/PKG00291485-instructions.pdf]

Note (judgment): Category F ("software ... designed to improve efficiency") and K ("improve financial stability") are the hooks for back-office/RCM tech; category D explicitly names AI but is framed around "care delivery". The NOFO's own "Financial solvency strategies" section asks states how they will stabilize rural hospitals financially.

### Caps / restrictions
- Provider payments (B) <= 15% of a state's award per budget period. [NOFO]
- EMR replacement <= 5% of award if a HITECH-certified EMR was in place as of Sept 1, 2025. [NOFO]
- Capital/infrastructure (J) <= 20%; state admin <= 10%. [CMS webinar slides via NRHA: https://www.ruralhealth.us/nationalruralhealth/media/documents/advocacy/2025/sapra-presentation-nrha-09-25-25.pdf]
- "Rural Tech Catalyst Fund"-like initiatives <= lesser of 10% or $20M. [NOFO]
- No supplanting: cannot pay for things already funded (e.g., existing software licenses per MN guidance); cannot pay for billable clinical services. [NOFO; MN NOGO]

#### Minnesota (MDH Office of Rural Health and Primary Care) - STRONGEST DIRECT FIT FOUND
- Administrator: "The MDH Office of Rural Health and Primary Care (ORHPC) is administering Minnesota's RHTP." Contact hospitals.ruraltransformation.mdh@state.mn.us. [https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/hospitalnogo.pdf]
- Structure: formula-based, non-competitive grants directly to hospitals. Hospitals get 70% of MN's award in BP1 and 45% in BP2-5; "Estimated Number of Awards 94; Estimated Maximum Award in Budget Period 1 $1,400,000". NOGO published 3/18/2026; rolling applications to 5/26/2026; agreements from May/June 2026 to Oct 30, 2030; BP1 funds spendable through Sept 30, 2027. [same]
- Initiative 5 "Invest in Technology, Infrastructure, and Collaboration for Financial Viability" allows: "Revenue cycle management and related tools to automate and optimize billing, coding, claims processing and payment collection ... Revenue cycle management tools are intended to improve billing processes by reducing claim denials and payment delays, thereby improving providers' overall financial performance." Also AI tools (framed as clinical documentation/scribes). [same]
- Vendor selection: "Providers will go through their own procurement processes to select the vendors of their choice." "MDH will review and approve proposed IT investments as part of grantees' work plans." Reporting checkbox includes "Revenue Cycle Management". [same]
- Constraints: no supplanting ("using RHTP funds to pay for existing software licenses" is ineligible); hospitals must use the Rural Health Value "Value-Based Care Assessment Tool"; RCM investments are framed as part of a path toward value-based care. [same]
- Implication (judgment): any of ~94 MN rural hospitals that selected Initiative 5 can pay a new denials/appeals tool with RHTP money in their own procurement, subject to MDH work-plan approval. Hospitals have already submitted BP1 work plans (deadline 5/26/2026); work plans and budgets for future budget periods are submitted annually, so the next insertion window is the BP2 work plan (BP2 starts Oct 31, 2026). Exact BP2 work-plan deadline: NOT_FOUND.

## Template summary: Federal/state funding: CMS Rural Health Transformation Program
- What it is / how hospitals buy through it: $50B (FY26-FY30, $10B/yr) CMS cooperative agreements to states under P.L. 119-21 Sec. 71401; hospitals cannot apply to CMS and receive money only via state subgrants, state-procured contractors/intermediaries, or formula allocations. Three buying patterns seen: (1) hospital receives a subgrant and procures its own vendor (MN, WA, MS, ID, SD, ND, WY tech challenge, TN); (2) the state or an intermediary procures a shared service that hospitals opt into (KS RTSO via Healthworks/Care Collaborative, MT CoE Implementation + shared-services/GPO, OK provider collaborative, ND Eide Bailly); (3) startup/"catalyst" funds that pay companies directly (NE NETECH, GA ARCHER; capped at lesser of 10% or $20M/yr per state). [NOFO; state sources above]
- Typical economics: grant $, not referral fees. Examples: MN up to $1.4M per hospital BP1 (formula); WA $200K-$500K per org; MS $200K-$400K per general tech award; ND $200K-$500K; KS RTSO expert team $1.8M Y1 statewide; MT shared vendor facilitation/GPO ~$1.23M Y1. Funds are reimbursement or obligation-based; no supplanting (cannot pay existing licenses); recurring SaaS fees must have a post-grant sustainability plan (WA, KS, MN). [state sources above]
- Realistic reach: every state; rural/CAH hospitals are primary targets. MN 94 eligible rural hospitals; KS 78 CAHs + 25 rural PPS; ID 26 CAHs; MS 75-100 tech awards. [MN NOGO; KS narrative; ID application; MS NOFO]
- Speed to first deal (judgment): 3-9 months per state, gated by the hospital's annual work-plan/budget cycle or the next solicitation. BP1 money is largely obligated by Oct 30, 2026; the next windows are BP2 (starts Oct 31, 2026; FY27 amounts announced by Oct 31, 2026) work plans, second-round RFAs (WI hospitals 2027, MS/SD/ND Y2 rounds), and intermediary shared-service rollouts (KS RTSO, MT CoE, OK collaborative) in late 2026/2027. Money for any BP must be spent within ~23 months, so tools that deploy fast win.
- Competitor risk: medium-high. Waystar, FinThrive, Quadax, SlicedHealth are marketing RHTP-funded RCM; Eide Bailly (ND), PYA and an unnamed RCM expert team (KS), Forvis Mazars (TN THA consultant) are embedded; KS is building a subsidized appeals/claims-recovery service that could substitute for Aegis. [sources in section 4]
- Partner motions that apply: fund (primary), contract (GPO/shared-services lists in MT, OK), endorse (association-run intermediaries such as KS Healthworks, MT MHA/MHREF), refer (TA providers like CO Rural Health Center, SORHs), integrate (EHR-enhancement grants in SD/ND/OR allow third-party add-on modules).
- Example orgs: MDH ORHPC (MN formula grants incl. RCM); Healthworks/KHA Foundation (KS RTSO); Montana DPHHS + MHA/MHREF (shared back-office vendors); ND HHS (AI and EMR enhancement grants); WA HCA (Provider Technology Fund). See candidate table.
- Taken categories: KS RTSO expert team (vendor NOT_FOUND); ND Eide Bailly (financial/revenue cycle assessments); KS PYA (rate analysis), Hospital Services Corporation (credentialing); CO Hospital Association with LateralCare (per RULES, not RHTP-specific).
- Vetting criteria found: MT "latest AI enabled technology, with a clearly demonstrated history of success with similar-sized providers", "favorable rates", SLAs and reporting; KS Emerging Tech "proven" technology with evidence in a comparable context, HIPAA/security compliance; WA sustainability of subscription costs; MN MDH approval of IT investments in work plans; universal 2 CFR 200 procurement standards for subrecipients.
- Notes/surprises: (1) The most explicit RCM/denials language is in MN, KS, MT, IA, MS, TN materials. (2) KS Emerging Tech explicitly excludes "principally back-office" AI, so RCM there must go through the Revenue Improvement Program instead. (3) Transparency on downstream vendors is weak; tracking who won intermediary RFPs requires state procurement portals.

## 5. Bottom line (judgment)
- Is it a real lever? Yes, but narrow and state-specific. RHTP is mainly a care-delivery, workforce and IT-infrastructure program; RCM/denials tools qualify only where a state wrote financial-sustainability technology into its plan. Strongest documented fits: Minnesota (explicit "reducing claim denials" line, hospital-chosen vendors), Kansas (state-funded denials/appeals service run through the hospital association's Healthworks), Montana (state-facilitated shared back-office vendors with an AI preference; Aegis's Glendive proof point is in-state), Mississippi, Washington, Idaho, Wyoming, Oklahoma, Oregon Phase 2, Wisconsin 2027 hospital round.
- How Aegis would access it: (a) Hospital-embedded: help CAH customers and prospects name Aegis in their subgrant work plans/budgets (MN BP2 work plans; WA BP2 work orders; MS/ND/SD/ID Y2 rounds; WI 2027), with ROI metrics (denial overturn rate, AR days) and a post-grant sustainability plan, and without supplanting existing licenses. (b) Intermediary subcontract: sell AI appeal-writing as the technology layer to the Kansas RTSO / Revenue Support Program awardee and Healthworks, the Montana CoE Implementation partner / MHA shared-services effort, and the Oklahoma provider collaborative's management vendor. (c) "Approved vendor": no federal list exists; closest are MT's shared-services/GPO route and preferred-vendor picks like ND's; register in state procurement portals (e.g., MT eMACS commodity code 2620) to get notices. (d) Catalyst funds (NE NETECH, GA ARCHER) could fund Aegis directly but favor consumer-facing/clinical tech (weak fit).
- Timing: Now to Q1 2027. FY27 awards land by Oct 31, 2026; BP2 work plans and second-round solicitations follow. Program ends with BP5 funds spendable to Sept 30, 2031; after that hospitals must pay from margin, so Aegis's ROI story (self-funding by recovered revenue) is the sustainability plan states ask for.
- Risks: annual rescoring could cut state awards; reimbursement-basis payments strain cash-poor CAHs; subsidized state appeals services (KS) could compete; large RCM vendors are already marketing RHTP.
