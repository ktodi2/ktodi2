# Phase 1 / B: State Offices of Rural Health + Flex/SHIP, and other public/philanthropic funders
Status: COMPLETE (researched 2026-09-24). All claims carry the URL they came from. "(judgment)" marks my own inference.
Cross-refs: RHTP is covered in depth in A_rhtp.md; networks (e.g., The Rural Collaborative) in E_networks_systems.md; CPA/consultants (Wipfli, Eide Bailly, Stroudwater, Wintergreen, HomeTown Health) in F_cpa_interim_coding.md.

## Bottom line (judgment)
1. The client hypothesis is CONFIRMED with sourced precedents: state Flex programs are *required* to run CAH Financial & Operational Improvement work, HRSA's own outcome menu names "reduced percentage of claims denied" and "increased percentage of denied claims re-billed", and at least one Flex program (Hawaii, May 2026) paid a vendor to build a CAH claims-denial dashboard. Others fund per-CAH revenue cycle projects (ND, WI), vetted-consultant portfolios (VA), cohort RCM projects (KS, IA, MT, MI, GA).
2. Ticket sizes are small: typically $11.5k to $18k per CAH project, cohort pools of $40k or so, and statewide Flex budgets of about $1M/yr. This is a **pilot-funding and credibility channel, not a revenue channel**. The value is (a) a subsidized first contract with a CAH, (b) an outcome metric (denial rate) that Flex programs must report to HRSA, and (c) introductions to every CAH CFO in the state.
3. SHIP (~$13.5k per hospital per year, ~1,600 hospitals) can pay for software subscriptions and training in the PB/PPS category, but "contractor/consulting/vendor consultation" services are unallowable. An outsourced appeals *service* likely does not fit; a software subscription + staff training packaging might, with Project Officer confirmation (judgment). Oregon already runs a SHIP-funded "Denials Analysis and Education" option (Wipfli).
4. Best first targets (judgment): Montana (Glendive tie; MHREF runs Flex FOI + Revenue Cycle Intensive; MT RHTP plan prioritizes AI-enabled back-office vendors), North Dakota (open per-CAH RCM subcontracts), Hawaii (denials precedent), Virginia (vetted portfolio, tech-enabled projects explicitly OK), Oregon (SHIP pooled vendor menu), Kansas (KRHOP denials cohort), Iowa/Georgia (HomeTown Health holds FOI/RISE contracts; partner or displace).

## Partner type 1: State Offices of Rural Health (SORH), state Flex programs, SHIP, and their national TA layer (TASC, FMT, NOSORH)

- **What it is / how hospitals buy through it:**
  - The Medicare Rural Hospital Flexibility (Flex) Program is a HRSA/FORHP cooperative agreement to states; SORHs carry it out; it serves 1,360 CAHs. [https://www.hrsa.gov/rural-health/grants/rural-hospitals/flex]
  - Five program areas; Area 2 "CAH Financial & Operational Improvement" is REQUIRED. All state Flex programs must assess CAH finances, identify higher-need CAHs, and implement interventions, with at least one improvement project with a measurable outcome per year. Project types include Revenue Cycle Management, Chargemaster Review, Billing and Coding Education. HRSA's example RCM outcomes: short term "reduced registration errors", intermediate "Reduced percentage of claims denied", "Increase percentage of denied claims re-billed", "Improved clean claims rate", long term "days' net revenue in AR" (CAHMPAS). FORHP "encourages states to identify new or existing successful financial and operational improvement programs" and leverage them. [https://www.ruralcenter.org/sites/default/files/FY24-FY28%20Flex%20Program%20Structure.pdf] (FY24-FY28 structure, updated 04/2025)
  - Flex fiscal year runs Sept 1 to Aug 31; current cycle FY2024-FY2028. [https://www.ruralcenter.org/sites/default/files/TASC%20Flex%20Fundamentals_9.2.26.pdf]
  - Money reaches vendors in three observed ways: (a) state RFP/RFQ for a vendor or consultant (HI, VA, MN, KY, AR, TX, KS), (b) per-CAH subcontracts/subgrants that the CAH uses to hire a consultant (ND, WI), (c) a standing contractor that runs FOI for the state (MT: MHREF/MHA; IA: HomeTown Health; KS: KRHOP/HealthWorks; UT: Utah Hospital Association; TN: Tennessee Hospital Association). Sources in state table below.
  - Some states do NOT sub-grant: Montana "Flex Program funding is provided to CAHs through access to education and programs developed by MT Flex and not directly allocated to a hospital via sub-grants." [https://mtpin.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Intro_Flex_22.pdf] (2022)
  - Eligibility gate: CAHs must meet MBQIP reporting to receive Flex-funded benefits (MT, WA examples). [https://mtpin.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Data_Flex_22.pdf] [https://doh.wa.gov/public-health-provider-resources/rural-health/rural-health-systems]
  - SHIP: SORH is fiscal intermediary; hospitals with 49 or fewer beds apply annually and buy hardware, software and training in three categories (VBP, ACO, PB/PPS). [https://www.hrsa.gov/grants/find-funding/HRSA-23-033] [https://www.ruralcenter.org/resources/ship-allowable-investments-spending-categories]
- **Typical economics:**

[https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/10/2026-18425/notice-of-supplemental-funding-medicare-rural-hospital-flexibility-program] (Note: FY2026 President's budget had proposed eliminating Flex [https://www.khi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/FORHP-Grant-Spotlight-Medicare-Rural-Hospital-Flexibility-Program-Web.pdf]; Congress instead increased it.)
  - Example state Flex award: Minnesota $1,049,465/yr. [https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/funding/grants/docs/flexbctrfp.pdf]
  - Vendor-facing ticket sizes: HI denial dashboard NTE $18,000 [https://www.govcb.com/government-bids/CRITICAL-ACCESS-HOSPITAL-CLAIMS-AND-23616862.htm]; ND CAH subcontracts $11,550 each, $40,000 pool [https://ruralhealth.und.edu/projects/flex/funding-programs/awards] [https://www.ruralhealthinfo.org/funding/2253]; WI up to $15,000 per hospital for consultant financial analysis [https://worh.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/D2H-Finance-Analysis-Grant-2025-26.pdf]; MN up to $15,000/yr for a billing/coding training organization [https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/funding/grants/docs/flexbctrfp.pdf]; VA no preset cap, priced per 1 / 2 / all 9 CAHs [https://vrha.org/flex/].
  - SHIP: FY2026 estimated max $13,528 per hospital (MN, TX) [https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/funding/grants/docs/shipnogo.pdf] [https://texasagriculture.gov/Portals/0/Publications/ER/SORH/SHIP/2026_-_SHIPRFGA1.5.2026.pdf]; FY2023 $20.7M to 46 states, 1,660 hospitals, ~ $13,312 each; PB/PPS category = 29.3% of funds [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CMR-HE20_9000-00198896/pdf/CMR-HE20_9000-00198896.pdf].
  - No referral fee or royalty model exists here; these are grant-funded purchases under federal cost principles (judgment).
- **Realistic reach:** 45 Flex states, ~1,360 CAHs [HRSA Flex page above; Fed Reg above]; SHIP ~1,600 small rural hospitals in 46 states [https://www.hrsa.gov/rural-health/grants/rural-hospitals/ship]. Per-state SHIP counts FY2023: MT 46, ND 36, SD 42, MN 61, KS 94, IA 78, NE 63, WI 66, TX 118, GA 56, WA 43 [govinfo SHIP report above].
- **Speed to first deal (judgment):** 3 to 9 months. The FY2026-27 Flex year started Sept 1, 2026 with work plans already set, but several states run mid-year competitive rounds (ND released Dec 8, due Jan 9 [https://ruralhealth.und.edu/projects/cah-quality-network/newsletter/2025-fall]; HI RFQ open 10 days in May; VA portfolio RFP due Aug 28, 2026 [https://vrha.org/flex/]). SHIP hospital applications are typically due in January for a June 1 start (MN [https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/funding/grants/docs/shipnogo.pdf]; TX Jan 2026). Getting written into FY2027-28 work plans means engaging coordinators by spring 2027.
- **Competitor risk: LOW to MEDIUM.** SORHs themselves sell nothing. But the FOI slots are occupied by consultants who do RCM assessments/education: HomeTown Health, Wipfli, Eide Bailly, Stroudwater, Wintergreen, ArchPro Coding (sources below). None found selling AI appeal-writing software in these channels (judgment, based on sources reviewed). Risk rises if an incumbent consultant also offers outsourced RCM (not verified here; see F_cpa_interim_coding.md).
- **Partner motions that apply:** fund (primary: Flex FOI projects, SHIP software/training), endorse (inclusion on a state's vetted vendor/pooled-option menu: OR, VA), refer (SORH/Flex coordinators introduce CAH CFOs; low-formality), integrate (none). Resell/contract: no.

- **Realistic reach:** DRCHSD ~10 orgs/yr in 252 Delta counties (8 states); ARH-TAC >=5 orgs/yr in 13 states; RHSP 8 hospitals in first cohort; USDA/NRHA initially 32 hospitals targeted (2022 slide). GA HEART ~57 hospitals [https://www.georgiaheart.org/hospitals/page/hospitals-by-financial-need]; TX HHSC served 108 rural hospitals in FY2024 [TX report above].
- **Speed to first deal (judgment):** 6-18 months. TA programs select hospitals first, then consultants make recommendations; Aegis would enter as an implementation option after an FOA flags denials. DSIP is annual (NOFO in winter, awards Sept).
- **Competitor risk: MEDIUM.** Consultant networks (Stroudwater did DRCHSD FOAs and a Delta Memorial Hospital revenue cycle assessment that flagged denials and the hospital's external revenue cycle vendor [https://www.stroudwater.com/case-study/from-fragmented-processes-to-a-more-accountable-revenue-cycle/]) control recommendations; they may steer to their own services (judgment).
- **Partner motions that apply:** fund (DSIP, TX grants, RHTP), refer (TA consultants recommend vendors), endorse (RHSP "vendor demonstrations"). Resell/contract/integrate: no.
- **Vetting criteria:** RHSP excludes hospitals in other overlapping TA programs; readiness requires permanent CEO and CFO. [https://www.ruralcenter.org/programs/stabilization/application-analysis-and-selection-process] [https://www.ruralcenter.org/programs/stabilization/faq]. DSIP requires prior TA recommendations. [HRSA-25-032 above]
- **Notes / surprises:** Helmsley and CoBank are low fit for RCM software (capital/equipment focus). The strongest non-SORH lever is RHTP (see A_rhtp.md) and Texas HHSC state grants that hospitals can spend flexibly (judgment).
