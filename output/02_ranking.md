# 02. Ranking the partner types

Scores run 1 (poor) to 5 (strong). Every score is our judgment, built from the sourced facts in `01_landscape.md`.

## How we scored

| Criterion | What earns a 5 |
|---|---|
| **Reach** | Many rural, CAH or community hospitals per partner, or across the partner type |
| **Trust** | Each relationship carries real weight with the CFO: they are a board, owner, auditor or funder |
| **Fit** | The partner complements an AI denials product. Scores fall where the partner sells denials or appeals itself, or where a competitor holds the slot. |
| **Speed** | A first signed hospital within about 3 months of the first conversation |
| **Cost** | Cheap to Aegis: small or no fees, or the partner pays (grants) |

The total is out of 25 and unweighted. We checked the order under a weighting that doubles Fit and Trust. It barely changes, so we show the simple total.

## The top 6 types to go deep on

### 1. CPA and advisory firms (21)
- **Why they rank first.** The auditor and cost-report preparer is the one outside advisor every CAH CFO talks to every year.
- **Reach.** CLA alone says it works with 900+ hospitals, and Plante Moran prepares 1,200+ cost reports a year.
- **Fit.** These firms advise on denials but mostly do not run appeals, so Aegis becomes the "implementation" answer to their revenue cycle assessments. FTI plus Penguin Ai shows the model works, and that a competitor may take it first.

### 2. Rural networks, hospital-owned co-ops and their group RFPs (21)
- **Why they rank here.** Networks are governed by the member hospitals' own CEOs and CFOs. They run the revenue cycle peer groups where denials get discussed, and some issue group contracts.
- **Live contract.** The Rural Collaborative / Rural Health Enterprise RFP in Washington explicitly buys appeals.
- **Montana.** Montana Health Network and the Yellowstone network both count Glendive as a member or owner.

### 3. State Offices of Rural Health / Flex / SHIP (20)
- **Why they rank here.** HRSA's own Flex outcome menu measures "reduced percentage of claims denied". Hawaii already paid for a denial dashboard.
- **What it is good for.** The money is small, but a Flex coordinator can put Aegis in front of every CAH in a state as a funded pilot and report the results to HRSA. That makes it the credibility engine, not the revenue engine.

### 4. CMS Rural Health Transformation Program (19)
- **Why it ranks here.** It is the only channel where a third party pays.
- **Best states:** Minnesota (hospital-chosen revenue cycle tools that reduce "claim denials"), Kansas (state-funded appeals support), Montana (AI-preferred shared back-office vendors), Washington and Mississippi.
- **Caveats.** It works in roughly 10 states, and the window is tied to budget-period work plans through early 2027.

### 5. State hospital association service arms (18)
- **Why they rank here.** An endorsement has the highest trust per relationship of any channel, which is why v1 targeted associations.
- **Why they are not higher.**
  - About 10 states already endorse a denials vendor.
  - Many programs are exclusive per category.
  - Endorsement takes 3-9 months.
- **How to work them.** Rank states by open slot plus CAH count, and use AES as the multi-state door.

### 6. Complementary finance-office vendors (18)
- **Who they are.** Patient-payment firms (AblePay, ClearBalance, CarePayment, PatientPay), cost-report and decision-support software (HFS, CostFlex, Toyon Absolute), and coding-only firms (HIA, HMI, ArchPro).
- **Why they rank here.** They sell to the same CFO with no appeals product, and several already hold association endorsements.
- **What the deal looks like.** Co-referral: reciprocal, low-cost and fast. Montana precedent: Livingston HealthCare moved patient balances to ClearBalance in Aug 2026.

## Types to ignore (for now), and why

- **EHR vendor partner programs (MEDITECH Alliance, Oracle Marketplace).**
  - MEDITECH (Claim Denial Agents, March 2026) and Oracle Health (appeal management, announced 2026-09-23) now have native appeal drafting. A marketplace listing puts Aegis next to the vendor's own agent.
  - Keep the technical integrations: Greenfield access and the Acmeware OneLink data feed. Drop the channel ambition.
  - Revisit in 2027 once it is clear how good the native agents are.
- **Clearinghouses and claims tech.** Most have their own denial modules, long partner cycles and no incentive to refer. Exception: SSI, which touches most rural MEDITECH-as-a-Service sites, is worth one exploratory conversation.
- **National GPOs.** HealthTrust requires a "national in scope" sales force. Awards take 9-18 months and only give permission to sell, not deals. Use regional co-op RFPs instead (folded into type 2).
- **Cibolo-managed networks.** Cibolo sells denial management and names TruBridge its preferred RCM partner. Approach member hospitals and network committees (through type 2), not Cibolo corporate.
- **Coding, CDI and physician-advisor firms (broad).** Aspirion, AGS, Enjoin, Sound and Revint already sell appeals. Keep only the coding-only firms (in type 6).
- **Operators, PE owners and turnaround firms.** Those that centralize RCM use R1, Amplify or in-house teams. Avoid American Healthcare Systems, which is in fraud litigation.
- **Malpractice insurers and brokers.** No hospital revenue cycle vendor programs exist, and Curi now sells RCM itself.
- **Lenders (USDA, CoBank, state facility finance authorities).** They finance capital, not software choices.
- **Health systems with rural affiliate programs.** Deprioritized, not ignored.
  - Affiliates are moving to Epic Community Connect (about 70% of small-hospital decisions in 2024), and several systems run affiliate billing.
  - Exception: Billings Clinic-Logan Health (Oracle Health, 16 partnerships, Glendive is an affiliate). It carries into Phase 3-4 as a named target inside the Montana cluster.
- **Interim leadership (17) and RCM consultancies / management companies (16).**
  - These just missed the cut.
  - Interim CFOs are strong champions but one hospital at a time.
  - Management companies (Ovation, CHC, HealthTech) sell RCM. v1 already covers the rural RCM consultancies.
  - Use independent interim CFOs opportunistically as warm intros.
